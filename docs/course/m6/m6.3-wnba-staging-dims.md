# M6.3 · WNBA staging models and conformed dims

> **Ticket:** [#85](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/85) ·
> **Mode:** agent: the agent drafts, you review ·
> **Time:** 3 to 4 hours · **Needs:** M5 merged (NBA staging, dims, facts, tests), M6.2

## Why this matters

**Conformed dimensions** are the reason a dimensional model is worth
the effort: one `dim_team`, one `dim_season`, one `fact_team_game`,
whatever the league. A question like "home win % by league since 2015"
then becomes one `GROUP BY`, not two pipelines glued together in a
dashboard.

Adding a second league is the test of whether M3 and M5 really
built conformed dims, or NBA tables with generic names. The WNBA's
**summer calendar** is the sharpest edge: anything that assumed "a
season runs October to June" breaks here.

## What you'll produce

- `stg_wnba__*` staging models mirroring your `stg_nba__*` models.
- Dims and facts extended so WNBA rows flow into the **same** facts
  (`fact_team_game`, `fact_play`, …) with a `league` column.
- `dim_season` handling both calendars.
- League-level tests, passing.

## Steps

### 1. Audit the NBA assumptions (45 min, by hand)

Search your dbt project for things that only hold for the NBA:

```bash
grep -rniE "nba|october|'10'|-10-|82|season_type" transform/models transform/snapshots | less
```

Look especially for:

- a season derived from a date (`year(game_date) + 1 if month >= 10`)
- surrogate keys built without `league` (`md5(team_id)` collides: ESPN
  team 14 is the Heat *and* the Storm)
- tests with NBA constants (`accepted_values` on 30 team abbreviations,
  82 games per team)
- source definitions or freshness thresholds tuned to an October to
  June calendar

✅ **Checkpoint:** a written list of every assumption, with file and line.
This list is half the prompt.

### 2. Design `dim_season` for two calendars (30 min, by hand)

This is the one design decision in the ticket. Do it yourself.

| League | Season label | Regular season (roughly) | Label rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| NBA | `2026` = 2025-26 | October to April | Year the season **ends** |
| WNBA | `2026` = 2026 | May to September | **Calendar** year |

Decide:

- **The grain.** One row per (league, season)? Then `season_key`
  includes league.
- **Display names.** `"2025-26"` for NBA, `"2026"` for WNBA, so nobody
  joins NBA 2026 to WNBA 2026 thinking they're the same period.
- **Phase dates.** Regular season and playoff start/end per row, taken
  from the schedule data rather than hard-coded.
- **How a game finds its season.** From the source's `season` column
  (preferred: the source knows) rather than deriving it from the date.

<details>
<summary>Hint · A dim_season shape that works for both</summary>

```
season_key        -- e.g. hash of (league, season)
league            -- 'nba' | 'wnba'
season            -- source's season number
season_label      -- '2025-26' or '2026'
regular_start_date, regular_end_date
playoffs_start_date, playoffs_end_date
```

Derive the dates from the schedule: `min(game_date)` and `max(game_date)`
per `(league, season, season_type)`.

</details>

### 4. Write the league-level tests yourself first

The agent will write tests, but decide what "correct" means yourself. A
starting list:

- `unique` on every fact's grain **including** league
  (e.g. `(league, game_id, team_id)` on `fact_team_game`)
- each game has exactly two team rows, one winner (per league)
- `relationships`: every fact row's `season_key` and `team_key` exists in the dims
- per league and season, regular-season games per team within a sane
  range. Don't hard-code 82: the WNBA schedule length has changed over
  the years. A range or a seed table of expected counts both work.
- `accepted_values` on `league`

### 5. Review the draft

| Review question | What a good answer looks like |
|---|---|
| Does any surrogate key omit `league`? | No: grep every `generate_surrogate_key`/`md5` call |
| Did NBA row counts change? | Identical before and after (compare `select count(*)` per model, NBA only) |
| How does a game get its season? | From the source `season` column, joined to `dim_season` on `(league, season)` |
| Are WNBA staging types identical to NBA's? | Same column types, so the `UNION ALL` doesn't silently cast |
| Do incremental models still work for NBA? | Their `is_incremental()` filters weren't loosened to full refreshes |
| Did any test get weakened to pass? | Diff the schema YAML: no `severity: warn` added, no tests removed |

## Gotchas

- **Accidental cross-league joins.** A join on `team_id` alone between
  WNBA facts and NBA dims "works" and returns nonsense. A
  `relationships` test on keys that include league catches it.
- **Season 2026 exists in both leagues** and means different months.
  Never filter a date range by season number across leagues.
- **The Commissioner's Cup final** (WNBA) is like the NBA Cup final: a
  game with unusual standings treatment. Check how your source labels it.
- **Full refresh cost.** Adding a column to an incremental fact may need
  `--full-refresh`. Note it in the PR so you remember why history was rebuilt.
