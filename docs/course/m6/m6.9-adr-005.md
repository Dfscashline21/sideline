## Why this matters

An ADR (architecture decision record) is a short document that records a
decision, the context, the options and the trade-offs, so that someone
joining later knows *why*. Senior engineers are judged on decisions, and
an ADR is a decision in writing. Interviewers like ADRs because they show
whether you weigh options or just pick favourites.

This one is unusual: you have hands-on evidence for **both** options.
Use it.

## What you'll produce

`docs/adr/005-dagster-vs-airflow.md`.

## Steps

### 1. Reuse your ADR format (10 min)

Open ADR-001 to ADR-004. Use the same headings, so the ADR index (PF.3)
is consistent. If you don't have a template yet, this is the common
Nygard-style shape:

```markdown
# ADR-005: Dagster as Sideline's orchestrator (Airflow 3 considered)

- Status: Accepted
- Date: YYYY-MM-DD
- Deciders: <you>

## Context
## Decision drivers
## Options considered
## Decision
## Consequences
## Evidence
```

### 2. Write the context (15 min)

What does Sideline need from an orchestrator? Pull from M6's tickets:
date × league partitions, two league calendars, event-driven runs after
games, blocking quality checks, backfills alongside daily runs,
dbt integration, one person operating it.

### 3. Rank the decision drivers (15 min)

Pick four or five drivers and **order** them. The order is the decision.
For example (yours may differ): partition and backfill support, dbt
integration, local dev and testing loop, operational weight, hiring-market
familiarity.

### 4. Options (30 min)

For each option (Dagster; Airflow 3; optionally "cron + scripts" as the
baseline), a short paragraph and a pros/cons list. **Every claim should
point to evidence** from M6.4 to M6.8: a line count, a timing, a screenshot,
a step you struggled with. Generic claims ("Airflow is mature") are fine
only as context.

<details>
<summary>Hint · What strong evidence looks like</summary>

- "Blocking publish on a failed check was one argument (`blocking=True`)
  in Dagster; in Airflow it relied on the default `all_success` trigger
  rule, which also skips publish when *ingest* fails, so the two failure
  modes look the same in the UI." (if that's what you saw)
- "Backfilling a week: one UI action in both; Airflow 3's `backfill
  create` ran through the scheduler, unlike Airflow 2's long-running CLI."
- "The date × league grid is native in Dagster; in Airflow it was one DAG
  per league, scheduled independently."

</details>

### 5. Decision and consequences (20 min)

State the decision in one sentence. Then the consequences, **including
the negative ones**: what you give up, what would make you revisit it
(e.g. "joining a team with a large Airflow estate"; "needing an
orchestrator managed by a cloud provider").

### 6. Read it as a stranger (10 min)

Could someone who wasn't there understand why, in under five minutes? Cut
anything that's diary rather than decision.
