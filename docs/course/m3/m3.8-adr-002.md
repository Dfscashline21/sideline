# M3.8 · ADR-002: Kimball over Data Vault

> **Ticket:** [#53](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/53) ·
> **Mode:** hand · **Time:** 90 min ·
> **Needs:** M3.1 session 4, M3.7 (the critique gives you objections to answer)

## Why this matters

An Architecture Decision Record (ADR) is a short document that captures
**one** significant decision: the context, the options, what you chose
and what it costs you. Teams keep them so that, a year later, nobody
asks "why on earth did we do it this way?" without an answer.

In interviews, "tell me about a technical decision you made and its
trade-offs" is guaranteed. An ADR is that answer, written down. Your
second ADR (after ADR-001 in M2.8) uses the same format, so by PF.3 you
have a consistent index of them.

## What you'll produce

`docs/adr/002-kimball-over-data-vault.md`, about one page.

## The format

The widely used template from Michael Nygard, which most teams adapt:

```markdown
# ADR-002: Kimball dimensional modeling over Data Vault

- Status: Accepted
- Date: YYYY-MM-DD
- Deciders: <you>

## Context
What problem, which forces and constraints apply. Facts, not opinions.

## Options considered
1. ...
2. ...
3. ...

## Decision
What we chose, in one or two sentences.

## Consequences
What becomes easier, what becomes harder, what we must now do,
and what would make us revisit this.
```

If ADR-001 used a slightly different template, match it. Consistency
across ADRs matters more than the exact headings.

## Steps

### 1. Write the context (20 min)

State the forces that make this a real decision. Pull them from your
requirements (M3.7 step 1) and real numbers where you have them:

- number of sources, and how often their schemas change
- number of leagues and how different they are
- who consumes the model (analyst SQL, dashboard, API, ML features)
- team size (one engineer) and audit requirements (none regulatory)
- what M3.2 to M3.6 already designed

<details>
<summary>Hint · Nudge: keep opinions out of Context</summary>

"Data Vault is too complex" is an opinion. "Sideline has four sources,
one engineer, and consumers that query with SQL and BI tools" is a fact
that *leads* to the same conclusion. Context is facts; the Decision
section draws the conclusion.

</details>

### 2. List the options fairly (20 min)

At least three, each with an honest strength and weakness:

| Option | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Kimball star schema | ? | ? |
| Data Vault 2.0 (with marts on top) | ? | ? |
| One Big Table | ? | ? |

Optional fourth: Inmon-style normalised (3NF) warehouse.

A strawman option ("Data Vault, which is bad") convinces nobody. Give
each option the strongest case its fans would make. M3.1's exercise 4.1
paragraph is your starting point.

### 3. Write the decision (10 min)

One or two sentences. Name what you chose **and** any part of a rejected
option you're keeping. For example, many teams keep an untouched raw
layer (Data Vault's "load everything, change nothing" spirit) under a
Kimball model. Your raw zone from M2 already does that.

### 4. Write the consequences (30 min)

Both columns, honestly:

- **Easier:** simple joins for analysts and the dashboard; SCD2 handles
  trades and realignment; dbt and its testing fit naturally.
- **Harder:** what happens when a source changes its schema; adding a
  fifth source; auditing exactly which load produced a number.
- **Must do now:** e.g. keep raw data unchanged (ADR-001), test grain
  uniqueness (M9), document conformed dimensions.
- **Revisit if:** concrete triggers, e.g. "more than ~10 sources", "a
  regulatory audit requirement", "multiple teams loading in parallel".

Answer at least one objection from your M3.7 critique log here.

<details>
<summary>Hint · What a "revisit if" trigger looks like</summary>

Weak: "if requirements change". Strong: "if Sideline integrates more
than ten sources whose schemas change monthly, the cost of
re-conforming dimensions on every change would exceed Data Vault's join
overhead." A trigger someone could actually check.

</details>

## Gotchas

- **Too long.** An ADR is one page. If you're past two, you're writing
  a design doc. Link to `data-model.md` instead of repeating it.
- **Rewriting history.** Once accepted, an ADR isn't edited. If you
  change your mind later, write a new ADR that *supersedes* it and set
  the old one's status to "Superseded by ADR-00N".
