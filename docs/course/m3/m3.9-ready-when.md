# M3.9 · Ready-when check (closed-book)

> **Ticket:** [#54](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/54) ·
> **Mode:** hand, closed-book · **Time:** 45 min ·
> **Needs:** M3.1 to M3.8 done

## Why this matters

The M3 promise: *given a new domain, name the grain, facts and
dimensions in 10 minutes.* "Model X for me" is a standard data
engineering interview round, and the domain is never the one you
practised. Sports was your practice domain; this check uses a different one.

## The rules

- **No AI, no notes, no design docs** until the timer stops.
- Paper or a whiteboard. Talk out loud as you would to an interviewer.
- Record yourself if you can. The recording shows where you hesitated.

## Steps

### 1. Pick a domain

The ticket names **ticket sales**. If you've already thought it through
in detail, pick one of the sealed alternatives instead. Choose without
reading the others; save them for IP.4's biweekly modelling practice.

<details>
<summary>Domain A · Ticket sales</summary>

A company sells tickets to concerts and sports events at many venues.
Customers buy one or more tickets per order, at different price tiers
and seats. Tickets can be refunded or resold on the company's own
marketplace. The business wants revenue by event, venue and day;
sell-through (% of seats sold) over time before each event; and resale
markup.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Domain B · Food delivery</summary>

Customers order from restaurants through an app; couriers pick up and
deliver. An order goes through placed → accepted → picked up →
delivered (or cancelled). The business wants delivery times by stage,
courier utilisation, and revenue by restaurant and neighbourhood.
Restaurants change their menus and prices often.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Domain C · Gym memberships</summary>

A gym chain sells memberships (monthly, annual, class packs) across
locations. Members check in and book classes; instructors teach classes.
Members upgrade, downgrade, freeze and cancel. The business wants
monthly recurring revenue, churn, class attendance by instructor, and
check-ins by location and hour.

</details>

### 2. Model it: 10 minutes, timed

Start the timer as you start reading. Follow the four steps out loud:

1. **Business processes:** list them. Pick the one or two that matter most.
2. **Grain:** one sentence per fact table, including its key.
3. **Dimensions:** for each, at least three attributes, and which need
   SCD Type 2.
4. **Facts:** measures, each marked additive, semi-additive or non-additive.

Then answer one question you'd expect as a follow-up:
- A: how do you model sell-through over time?
- B: which fact type fits an order moving through stages?
- C: how do you compute churn for a given month?

### 3. Check it

Timer off. Now compare with your notes and the Toolkit:

- Did each fact table have exactly one grain, stated before its facts?
- Did you spot the accumulating snapshot (B), the periodic snapshot
  (A's sell-through, C's monthly membership state), or did you force
  everything into transaction facts?
- Did you mark at least one Type 2 attribute (A: venue capacity or
  price tiers; B: restaurant menu prices; C: membership plan)?

### 4. Score yourself honestly

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Grain, facts and dimensions sound, in ≤ 10 min, follow-up answered | **Pass.** Close the ticket. |
| Sound but 10 to 20 min | Not yet. Redo M3.1 exercises 1.1 and 2.1 from a blank page, retry another domain in 2 days. |
| Grain unclear or facts at mixed grains | Repeat M3.2 on a new table set, then retry. |
