# M3.7 · Agent critique pass on the model

> **Ticket:** [#52](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/52) ·
> **Mode:** agent: the agent critiques, you judge ·
> **Time:** 90 min · **Needs:** M3.6 merged (the full model is written down)

## Why this matters

Senior engineers get their designs reviewed, and review other people's.
The skill here isn't asking an agent. It's **judging** its critique:
agents produce confident, generic advice ("consider adding indexes",
"normalise this") alongside real insight. Accepting everything is as
wrong as rejecting everything. Every accept or reject needs a reason
tied to *your* requirements.

This is also practice for M3.8, where you defend the overall approach.

## What you'll produce

A **Critique log** in `docs/notes/m3.md`: at least 3 real problems the
agent raised, each with your **accept** or **reject** decision and a
reason. Accepted ones are fixed in the design docs.

## Steps

### 1. Prepare the context (10 min)

The agent can only critique what it can see. Make sure it reads:

- `docs/design/data-model.md` (grains, dimension designs)
- `docs/design/player-crosswalk.md`
- `docs/design/erd.md`
- a short statement of requirements: three leagues (NBA, WNBA, men's
  and women's college), questions you want to answer, scale (millions of
  play-by-play rows a season), consumers (dbt marts, dashboard, API, ML
  features with point-in-time joins)

Write the requirements statement yourself if it doesn't exist yet. A
critique without requirements is just opinion.

### 2. Prompt for *problems*, not praise (10 min)

Adapt this prompt:

> Read docs/design/data-model.md, player-crosswalk.md and erd.md, and
> these requirements: *(paste)*.
>
> Find the **three most serious real problems** with this dimensional
> model: things that would produce **wrong numbers**, make a stated
> requirement impossible, or break when data arrives late or changes.
> For each, give a concrete example with specific rows or a query that
> goes wrong. Don't suggest stylistic changes, naming changes or
> performance tuning. Don't rewrite the model.

Asking for concrete failing examples is the important part. It turns
vague advice into something you can test.

### 3. Push back for depth (15 min)

For each critique, ask a follow-up:

> For problem 2, show the exact query and the input rows where my design
> gives the wrong answer.

If the agent can't produce a concrete failure, that's evidence for
rejecting it. If it asked for more than three, pick the three that
matter most and say why.

### 4. Judge each critique (30 min)

For each one, write:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Critique** | One sentence, in your words |
| **Example** | The failing case the agent gave (or "none provided") |
| **Decision** | Accept / Reject / Accept with changes |
| **Reason** | Tied to a requirement, a grain or a measured fact |
| **Change** | If accepted: what you changed, in which doc |

<details>
<summary>Hint · Critiques worth expecting (don't feed these to the agent)</summary>

Real issues a good critique tends to find in a design like the course
default:

- **Team on fact vs team on dim_player.** Two sources of truth for
  "which team". Accept: make the fact's `team_sk` the authority and say
  so. Or reject with a reason.
- **Late-arriving dimension changes.** A trade recorded a day late
  means facts loaded in between got the old `player_sk`. Does your
  design re-key them?
- **Stat corrections.** If the league corrects a rebound two days later,
  does `fact_play` update in place? How will incremental models (M5.6)
  pick that up?
- **Crosswalk merges.** When two `player_id`s turn out to be one person,
  what happens to facts and SCD2 history?

Typical critiques to **reject**, and why:

- "Use Data Vault for flexibility": not without many changing sources
  (that's M3.8's argument).
- "Normalise dim_team into team, city and conference tables":
  snowflaking costs joins and buys little at this size.
- "Add indexes": columnar engines (DuckDB, Iceberg) don't use them that way.

</details>

### 5. Apply the accepted changes

Update `data-model.md`, `player-crosswalk.md` and `erd.md` in the same
PR, so the log and the design agree.

✅ **Checkpoint:** each accept has a matching change in the design
docs; each reject has a reason a stranger could check.

## Review checklist (for the agent's output)

- [ ] Does each critique have a **concrete** failing example?
- [ ] Is it about **this** model, or generic advice that fits any model?
- [ ] Did the agent misread your grain or a design decision? (If so,
      that's a clarity problem in your docs. Worth fixing even if you
      reject the critique.)
- [ ] Is anything it calls a "problem" actually an explicit decision you
      made with a reason? Point to the reason.
