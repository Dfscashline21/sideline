## Why this matters

An ADR (Architecture Decision Record) captures **why** you chose
something, at the time you chose it, with the alternatives you rejected.
In interviews, "walk me through a technical decision and its trade-offs"
is answered best with one of these. In real teams, it's what stops the
same debate happening again in six months.

You made this decision in M5.6. Now write it down while the evidence is
fresh.

## Steps

### 1. Gather the evidence (20 min)

From M5.6's PR and notes:

- the strategy table you filled in (append / delete+insert / merge / microbatch)
- idempotency numbers (three runs, identical counts)
- correction results: 2 days late picked up, 5 days late not
- build time of a full refresh vs an incremental run (measure both if you
  didn't: `time make dbt-build` with and without `--full-refresh` on `fact_play`)

### 2. Find out how late corrections really are (30 min)

Your lookback of 3 days is a guess until you check it. If M2's raw zone
keeps every load, compare loads of the same game across days and count
how many days after the game the last change arrived.

If you don't have that history yet, say so honestly in the ADR, cite
what you know (the NBA reviews and corrects stats after games), and add a
follow-up: *revisit after a season of data*. An ADR that admits its
assumptions is stronger than one that hides them.

### 3. Write the ADR

Use the same headings as your earlier ADRs. If you don't have a template
yet, this is a common one:

```markdown
# ADR-004: Incremental strategy for fact_play

- Status: Accepted
- Date: YYYY-MM-DD
- Deciders: <you>

## Context
What fact_play is (grain, size, growth per season), how corrections arrive,
what a full rebuild costs today.

## Decision
Strategy, unique key, lookback window (and that it's a var), anchored on
max(game_date) in the target rather than current_date.

## Alternatives considered
One short paragraph each: append, merge, microbatch (and insert_overwrite,
noting dbt-duckdb doesn't support it). Why each was rejected *for this table*.

## Consequences
- Positive: ...
- Negative: corrections older than the window are missed; deletes upstream
  aren't propagated; ...
- How we'd notice: the tests that catch failure (reconciliation, unique).
- How to recover: --vars lookback override, or --full-refresh, and how long it takes.

## Evidence
Numbers from M5.6: idempotency, correction tests, timings.

## Revisit when
E.g. moving to a warehouse that supports insert_overwrite on partitions;
measured correction lag exceeds the window; fact_play passes N rows.
```

<details>
<summary>Hint · What makes an ADR good rather than long</summary>

- **Context** states forces, not history: size, correction lag, cost of
  wrong numbers, cost of slow builds.
- **Alternatives** are real options you could have picked, each with the
  specific reason it lost. "Merge was slower" needs a number, or "merge
  behaves the same here; chose delete+insert for portability" needs the
  portability reason.
- **Consequences** includes the bad ones. The missed 5-day correction is
  the most important sentence in this ADR.

</details>
