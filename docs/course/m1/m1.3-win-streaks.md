# M1.3 · Longest win streak per team (gaps and islands)

> **Ticket:** [#28](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/28) ·
> **Mode:** hand · **Time:** 2 to 3 hours ·
> **Needs:** M1.1 session 1, M1.2 (`nba_team_box`, `wnba_team_box`)

## Why this matters

"Gaps and islands" is the most common *hard* SQL interview pattern. Any
question shaped like "find consecutive runs of X" is this pattern:
login streaks, uptime periods, consecutive days above a threshold, win
streaks. Once you see the trick, you'll recognise it everywhere.

## What you'll produce

`sql/problems/001_longest_win_streak.sql`: for every NBA and WNBA team
since 2002, its longest win streak, with start and end dates. Pure SQL,
no loops, no procedural code.

## Steps

### 1. Pin down the question (decisions, written in the file header)

Before writing SQL, decide and write down:

- **Which games?** Regular season only, or include playoffs? (Mixing them
  creates "streaks" across a two-week gap and a different opponent pool.)
- **Within a season, or across seasons?** Golden State won their last 4
  of 2014-15 and first 24 of 2015-16. Is that a 28-game streak?
- **All-Star games?** They're in the data with `season_type = 2`, under
  made-up teams such as `Team LeBron` or `TEAM CLARK`. How will you spot
  them without listing every name?
- **The NBA Cup final?** It's `season_type = 2` but doesn't count in the
  standings. Keep it or drop it?

There's no single right answer, but your choice must be explicit. The
known-streak check in step 5 assumes **regular season, within a season**.

### 2. See the shape on one team

Get one team's games in order, with a W/L column:

```sql
select game_date, team_winner
from nba_team_box
where team_display_name = 'Miami Heat' and season = 2013 and season_type = 2
order by game_date;
```

On paper, mark the islands (runs of consecutive `true`). You want one row
per island with its length.

✅ **Checkpoint:** you can see the long run of wins starting in early
February 2013.

### 3. Find the trick

This is the core of the lab. Try for 20 minutes before opening hints.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1 · Nudge</summary>

You need a value that is **the same for every row in one island** and
**different between islands**. Then `GROUP BY` that value.

What happens if you number all of a team's games 1, 2, 3, … and,
separately, number only its *wins* 1, 2, 3, …?

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2 · Approach</summary>

Two `row_number()`s:

- `rn_all`: position among **all** the team's games (ordered by date)
- `rn_result`: position among games with the **same result** (partition
  also by `team_winner`)

Inside a winning streak both counters go up by 1 per game, so
`rn_all - rn_result` stays constant. A loss bumps `rn_all` but not the
win counter, so the next streak gets a different constant.

Work it on paper for W W L W W W:

| game | result | rn_all | rn_result | diff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | W | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | W | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| 3 | L | 3 | 1 | 2 |
| 4 | W | 4 | 3 | 1 |
| 5 | W | 5 | 4 | 1 |
| 6 | W | 6 | 5 | 1 |

Wins with diff 0 form one island (length 2); diff 1 is another (length 3).

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 3 · Skeleton</summary>

### 4. Write it for all teams, both leagues

Combine both tables with `UNION ALL` and a literal `league` column.
Partition by **league and team**: ESPN reuses team IDs across leagues
(NBA 14 is the Heat, WNBA 14 is the Storm).

Return one row per team: its longest streak, with dates. If a team has two
streaks tied for its longest, decide whether to show both and say why.

<details>
<summary>Solution (open only after you have your own working answer)</summary>

```sql
-- Problem:  Longest regular-season win streak per team, NBA and WNBA, since 2002
-- Pattern:  gaps and islands (row_number difference)
-- Tables:   nba_team_box, wnba_team_box
-- Decisions: regular season only; streaks reset each season; All-Star games excluded
-- Verified: Miami Heat 2012-13 = 27 (2013-02-03 to 2013-03-25)
with all_games as (
    select 'NBA' as league, team_id, team_display_name, season, game_date, game_id, team_winner
    from nba_team_box
    where season >= 2002 and season_type = 2
    union all
    select 'WNBA', team_id, team_display_name, season, game_date, game_id, team_winner
    from wnba_team_box
    where season_type = 2
),
games as (
    -- All-Star "teams" play 1 to 10 games a season; real franchises play 20+.
    -- Filtering on that beats keeping a list of every All-Star team name.
    select *
    from all_games
    qualify count(*) over (partition by league, team_id, season) >= 20
),
grouped as (
    select *,
        row_number() over (partition by league, team_id, season order by game_date, game_id)
      - row_number() over (partition by league, team_id, season, team_winner
                           order by game_date, game_id) as grp
    from games
),
streaks as (
    select league, team_id, any_value(team_display_name) as team, season,
           count(*) as streak_len, min(game_date) as start_date, max(game_date) as end_date
    from grouped
    where team_winner
    group by league, team_id, season, grp
)
select *
from streaks
qualify rank() over (partition by league, team_id order by streak_len desc) = 1
order by league, streak_len desc;
```

`rank()` rather than `row_number()` returns every tied longest streak for
a team. Notice `team_display_name` uses `any_value`: franchises rename and
relocate (Seattle SuperSonics → Oklahoma City Thunder), so grouping by
name instead of `team_id` would split them. Was that the right call for
your question?

</details>

## Gotchas

- **Non-deterministic ordering.** Two rows for one team on the same
  `game_date` can't happen in real life, but if data is ever duplicated,
  `order by game_date` alone gives arbitrary row numbers and wrong
  islands. Adding `game_id` as a tiebreaker makes it deterministic. Make
  this a habit.
- **Partitioning by result.** Forgetting `team_winner` in the second
  `row_number()`'s partition is the most common bug. The diff is then
  always 0 and every team has one giant "streak".
- **Relocations.** `team_id` is stable across renames in this data;
  names aren't.

## Stretch

- Streaks **across seasons** (Golden State's 28). What changes? Only the
  partitions, but check the gap between seasons doesn't break anything.
- Longest **losing** streak. It's a one-word change if your query is right.
- Same problem, solved with `LAG` and a running `SUM` instead (you'll learn
  that pattern in M1.5). Which is easier to read?
