# PF.7 · Site and LinkedIn update

> **Ticket:** [#176](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/176) ·
> **Mode:** hand · **Time:** 2 to 3 hours ·
> **Needs:** PF.1 (README), PF.5 (video), PF.6 (bullets); published blog
> posts (M4.9, M7.11, M12.8)

## Why this matters

Recruiters search LinkedIn, and many hiring managers check it before the
resume. If your profile doesn't mention the project, the 24 weeks of
work is invisible to anyone who didn't get your resume. The ticket's
criterion: **the profile links to the repo, the dashboard and the posts.**

## What you'll produce

- LinkedIn profile updated: headline/about mention the project; a
  featured section (or equivalent) linking the repo, live dashboard,
  demo video and blog posts; a project entry using your PF.6 bullets.
- Your personal site (if you have one) with a project page linking the
  same things.
- One announcement post (optional but recommended).

### 2. Update LinkedIn (60 min)

LinkedIn's layout changes often, so look for these areas rather than
exact menu names:

- **Headline:** one phrase naming the role you want, e.g. "Data Engineer ·
  lakehouse, streaming, dbt". Don't overload it.
- **About:** two or three sentences pointing to the project: what it is,
  one number, the link.
- **Featured:** the repo, the dashboard, the video, and your best blog
  post. Featured items are the most visible links on a profile.
- **Projects** (or an Experience-style entry if your profile has no
  Projects section): title, dates, repo link, and your PF.6 bullets
  (shortened).
- **Skills:** add the technologies you really used, matching the job
  postings from PF.6.

✅ **Checkpoint:** view your profile as a member of the public (LinkedIn
offers a public view of your own profile). The repo, dashboard and at
least one post are reachable within one click.

### 3. Update your site (45 min, if you have one)

A project page with: the pitch, the embedded video, the architecture
diagram, links to repo, dashboard and posts, and the numbers table. Reuse
README text; consistency matters more than novelty. No site? Skip this;
the README is your site.

### 4. Write an announcement post (45 min, optional)

A short post about **what you learned**, not a feature list: one hard
problem, the lesson, a link. Posts that teach get read more than posts
that announce.

<details>
<summary>Hint · A structure that works</summary>

1. One line: what you built and over how long.
2. The hardest problem (e.g. late events in streaming) in two or three
   sentences, and the fix.
3. One number you're proud of.
4. Links: repo, demo, the relevant blog post.
5. A question to invite replies ("How do you handle X?").

</details>

### 5. Cross-link everything (15 min)

The README should link your blog posts; each blog post should link the
repo; your site and LinkedIn should link all of them. A reader can then
enter anywhere and find everything.

## Gotchas

- **Employer content.** Nothing about your current employer's systems in
  public posts (STAR material stays private).
- **Broken or private links.** Re-check after publishing; some platforms
  rewrite URLs.
- **"Open to work" settings** are a personal choice with visibility
  options. Decide deliberately, especially if your employer can see your
  profile.
