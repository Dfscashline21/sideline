# M10.13 · STAR story from your current job

> **Ticket:** [#143](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/143) ·
> **Mode:** hand · **Time:** 45 min ·
> **Prompt:** *A security, access or cloud-cost problem at work and what
> you'd change.*

The STAR format, word budgets and common mistakes are in the
[M0.9 lab](../m0/m0.9-star-story.md). Reread its table before starting.
This lab covers only what's specific to a security or cost story.

## Finding the story

Questions to jog memory:

- **Access:** a shared admin login or service account everyone used. A
  credential in a script, a repo or a spreadsheet. Someone who left the
  company and still had access. An analyst who could see data they
  shouldn't (salaries, customer emails).
- **Security:** a near miss: a public bucket, a leaked key, an
  over-permissioned role found during an audit. A compliance request
  (GDPR deletion, SOC 2 evidence) that was painful because nothing was
  tracked.
- **Cost:** a surprise bill. A warehouse or cluster left running over a
  weekend. A dashboard whose refresh cost more than anyone realised. A
  vendor bill nobody could attribute to a team.

The prompt says "and **what you'd change**". If you saw the problem but
didn't own the fix, the honest version is fine: the Action is what you
did (raised it, quantified it, proposed a fix), and the Result includes
what you'd do now, informed by M10.

## What makes a security or cost story strong

- **A number.** Dollars per month, % of the bill, number of people with
  access before and after, days a credential was exposed, hours to pass
  an audit.
- **The principle, named.** Least privilege, no long-lived credentials,
  masking at the source, tagging for attribution, alarms before spend.
  Naming it shows you generalise from the incident.
- **Proportionate.** A cost cut that weakened security, or a lockdown that
  blocked analysts for weeks, invites hard follow-ups. Mention the
  trade-off you weighed.
- **Nothing sensitive.** Never describe an unfixed vulnerability at your
  current employer, or details that identify systems. Generalise:
  "a production database", "a cloud storage bucket".

## Follow-ups to prepare

Write a one-line answer to each:

1. "How did you find out?" (Detection is half the story.)
2. "How did you make sure it couldn't happen again?" (A policy, a check in
   CI, an alarm: like your M10.6 wildcard guard or M10.9 budget.)
3. "How did you get people to accept the change?" (Security changes slow
   people down. How did you handle pushback?)

## Where to save it

Private notes alongside your other STAR stories, **never** this repo.
Close the issue with "Saved to private notes" and no details.

## Done when

- [ ] S, T, A, R in under 200 words
- [ ] Includes a number (cost, people with access, time exposed, hours saved)
- [ ] Nothing sensitive about your employer's current systems
- [ ] Saved to private notes, not the public repo
- [ ] Three follow-up answers drafted
