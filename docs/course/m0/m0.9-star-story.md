# M0.9 · STAR story from your current job

> **Ticket:** [#25](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/25) ·
> **Mode:** hand · **Time:** 45 min ·
> **Prompt:** *A time poor engineering hygiene (no CI, no environments,
> manual deploys) cost your team.*

## Why this matters

Sideline proves you can build. Behavioural interviews ask whether you've
**done it at work**, with real constraints and real people. Every module
ends with a STAR story that links the module's skill to your actual job.
By IP.1 you'll have a bank of them, all already drafted.

This is the first one, so this lab also teaches the format. Later STAR
labs point back here.

**Privacy:** the story is about your employer. It goes in your
**private** notes, never in this public repo.

## The format

| Part | Words | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **S**ituation | ~30 | Where, when, what system. Just enough context. |
| **T**ask | ~25 | What *you* were responsible for. Not the team. |
| **A**ction | ~100 | What *you* did, in steps. Most of the story. |
| **R**esult | ~40 | What changed, **with a number**, plus what you learned. |

Under 200 words total. Spoken aloud, that's about 90 seconds, which is
what interviewers want.

## Steps

### 1. Mine for candidates (10 min)

List every incident you can remember that fits the prompt, even loosely.
Don't filter yet. Questions to jog memory:

- When did a change break something that a test or CI would have caught?
- When did "works on my machine" cost hours or days?
- When did a manual deploy or a manual data fix go wrong?
- When did someone lose work, or overwrite someone else's?
- Where does onboarding a new person take far too long?

### 2. Pick one (5 min)

Pick the story where **you** acted, not where you watched. A smaller
story where you led the fix beats a big outage you only observed.

If you genuinely have no story where you fixed it, pick one where you
saw the cost, and make your Action what you did *afterwards* (proposed
CI, wrote the runbook, added the check). That's still honest.

### 3. Find the number (10 min)

The acceptance criteria require a number. Candidates: hours lost, number
of failed deploys, rows corrupted, people blocked, days to onboard,
incidents per month before and after.

Estimates are fine if you say so ("roughly two days of analyst time").
Invented precision isn't: "saved 37.4%" with nothing behind it will fall
apart under one follow-up question.

### 4. Draft in the table, then as prose (15 min)

Fill the four rows above with bullet points first. Then write it as
plain sentences, first person singular ("I"), past tense.

<details>
<summary>Hint · Common draft problems</summary>

- **Too much Situation.** If S is longer than A, cut S. The interviewer
  doesn't need your org chart.
- **"We" everywhere.** Interviewers are scoring *you*. Say "I" for what
  you did; "the team" only for what others did.
- **Action is one sentence.** Break it into 2 to 4 concrete steps: what
  you noticed, what you proposed, what you built, how you got buy-in.
- **Result has no number,** or no learning. End with one line on what
  you'd do differently or now do by default.

</details>

### 5. Say it out loud (5 min)

Record yourself on your phone. Over two minutes, or did you stumble?
Cut and re-record. Stories are spoken, not read.

### 6. Prepare one follow-up

Interviewers dig. Write a one-line answer to: *"What would you do
differently?"* and *"How did you convince people?"*

## Where to save it

Private notes, outside this repo: a notes app, a private doc, or a
private repo. Make one file or page that collects all STAR stories, since IP.1
will compile them.

Close the issue with a comment like "Saved to private notes" and **no
story details**. The issue is public.

## Done when

- [ ] S, T, A, R written, under 200 words
- [ ] Includes at least one number (time, cost, incidents, rows or people)
- [ ] Saved to private notes, not the public repo

## Check yourself

1. Which of your Action steps would a junior engineer *not* have thought to do?
2. If the interviewer says "and then what happened?", what do you say?
