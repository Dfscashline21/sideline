# M8.9 · Demo video

> **Ticket:** [#115](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/115) ·
> **Mode:** hand · **Time:** 2 to 3 hours (most of it rehearsal) ·
> **Needs:** M8.4 to M8.6 running together; M8.10 written (it shapes the narration)

## Why this matters

The M8 epic's portfolio artifact is a 2-minute video of the live
dashboard during a replayed game. Recruiters watch videos; they rarely
run `docker compose up`. A tight demo also forces you to explain the
system in two minutes, which is the same skill as the first two minutes
of a system design interview.

## What you'll produce

- A 2-minute screen recording of a replayed game, with live team stats
  and the win-probability line updating.
- The video uploaded somewhere public and linked from the README.

## Steps

### 1. Build the screen (by hand, 60 min)

You need one screen that shows the feed working. The simplest:

- a Streamlit page in `serving/dashboard/live.py` (agent help is fine for
  layout; it's presentation, not core logic) that reads
  `win-probability` and the live team stats table from M8.4, refreshing
  every second or two
- the score, the win-probability line over game time, and a small
  team-stats table
- one small panel showing the **DLQ count**: it's the most senior thing
  on the screen

The README's "Numbers" section (M0.6) can link to the same page later.

### 2. Script the two minutes (30 min)

Write the narration before recording. A structure that works:

| Time | Show | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Architecture diagram (one slide) | What it is: a historical game replayed as a live feed through Redpanda into Spark and a Python scorer, landing in Iceberg |
| 0:15–0:45 | Dashboard, replay starting at 10x | Score and win probability updating within a second of each event |
| 0:45–1:15 | A late event with chaos on | "This basket arrived 20 seconds late; the window was corrected." Point at the DLQ panel for one beyond the watermark |
| 1:15–1:40 | Iceberg table query after a restart | No duplicates after a forced restart: how |
| 1:40–2:00 | Close | One decision from ADR-006 and what you'd do next |

Choose the moment with care: a close fourth quarter gives the most
dramatic probability swing. Find one in the practice data first (a game
where the lead changed in the last two minutes) and check its own
lateness is within the watermark, as in M8.4.

### 3. Rehearse (45 min)

Run the full replay three times while talking. Make sure the late event
and the DLQ example happen on screen at a predictable time: use a fixed
`--seed`, or the scripted sequence from your M8.5 test.

### 4. Record (30 min)

- macOS: Cmd-Shift-5 records the screen; QuickTime for voice.
- Hide notifications, close unrelated tabs, increase font sizes.
- Record at a resolution that stays readable when shrunk to a phone screen.
- Two minutes means two minutes. Cut anything that doesn't serve the story.

### 5. Publish and link

Upload to YouTube (unlisted is fine) or similar. Add it to the README:
a thumbnail image linking to the video works well on GitHub, since
GitHub won't embed a YouTube player.

```markdown
[![Sideline live game demo](docs/img/m8-demo-thumb.png)](https://youtu.be/<id>)
```

PR with `Closes #115`.

## Gotchas

- **Don't record a run that cheats.** If chaos is off, don't say "with
  late data". Say exactly what's on screen.
- **Large files.** Never commit the video itself. The pre-commit
  large-file hook will block it, and the repo shouldn't carry it anyway.
- **Credentials on screen.** Terminal windows with `.env` values or the
  MinIO console login are easy to capture by accident. Check every frame.
