# M11.7 · Ready-when check (closed-book)

> **Ticket:** [#150](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/150) ·

> **Needs:** M11.1 to M11.6 done

## Why this matters

The M11 promise: *explain how you'd serve the same data to a BI tool, an
app and an ML model, and why each differs.* This is a common senior
interview prompt, often as the second half of a system design question.
Answer it from a blank page, out loud, with no notes.

## The rules

- No AI, no notes, no docs, no looking at your repo until the timer stops.
- Whiteboard or paper. Talk out loud; record yourself if you can.

## Steps

### 1. Draw it (10 minutes, timed)

One gold table, `fact_player_game`, three consumers:

1. An analyst exploring in a **BI tool**
2. A fan-facing **app** with a player page
3. The **ML model** from M12 needing a player's recent form at prediction time

Draw the path from the table to each consumer: any intermediate store,
cache or service, and where each lives.

### 2. Explain the trade-offs (10 minutes, timed)

For each consumer, say out loud:

- the serving pattern and why
- latency and freshness it needs
- query shape (ad-hoc vs key lookup vs entity features)
- what breaks first under load, and the fix
- one alternative you rejected

Then answer the two follow-ups an interviewer would ask:

- "Analysts and the app show different numbers for points per game. Why,
  and how do you prevent it?" (Think: shared definitions, a semantic layer,
  one read model built from one dbt model.)
- "Why can't the model just call your API for features?" (Think:
  training/serving skew, point-in-time correctness, batch vs online.)

### 3. Check yourself against your work

Timer off. Open M11.1's notes and your M11.2 to M11.5 decisions. What did
you miss or contradict?
