# M9.5 · Volume anomaly checks per league

> **Ticket:** [#123](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/123) ·
> **Mode:** hand · **Time:** 2 to 3 hours ·
> **Needs:** M9.4 (check pattern and `ops.check_results`), at least one
> full season of play-by-play per league

## Why this matters

Volume checks catch the failures that pass every row-level rule: a
half-loaded game, a day loaded twice, an API that quietly returns the
first page only. Every row is valid; there are just too few or too many.

The hard part is the **threshold**. Guess it ("flag games under 300
events") and you'll either miss real problems or page someone for every
fast-paced college game. The acceptance criterion says it plainly:
*thresholds derived from data, not guessed.*

## What you'll produce

- `quality/checks/volume_per_game.sql` (or a dbt test) that flags games
  whose event count is far outside **their league's** historical range.
- `quality/rules/volume_thresholds.csv` (or a table) **computed** from
  history by a query you commit, not typed in.
- A short write-up in the PR: the method, the numbers, how many games it
  flags on history, and what it *can't* catch.

## Steps

### 1. Look at the distribution (30 min)

Per league, count events per game and look at the shape:

```sql
with g as (
    select league, game_id, count(*) as n_events
    from stg_pbp_all
    group by 1, 2
)
select league, count(*) games,
       quantile_cont(n_events, [0.01, 0.05, 0.5, 0.95, 0.99]) as pctiles,
       min(n_events), max(n_events)
from g group by 1;
```

Then plot a histogram per league (DuckDB's `histogram()` or a quick
matplotlib chart). Questions for your notes:

- Is the distribution symmetric? Are both tails a problem, or only one?
- Overtime games have more events. Is that an anomaly? (No.) How will
  you stop overtime from inflating the counts?

<details>
<summary>Hint · Normalising for overtime</summary>

Count only **regulation** events: `period_number <= regulation_periods`,
joining the M9.4 `league_rules`. Overtime then can't push a normal game
over the threshold. (Events per minute played is the other option.)

</details>

✅ **Checkpoint:** you can state each league's median events per game
from your own query. On 2024-25 ESPN data, regulation-only medians were
NBA 468, WNBA 386, men's college 335, women's college 341.

### 2. Choose a robust method (30 min)

Mean ± 3 standard deviations is the textbook answer and a weak one: the
outliers you're trying to catch inflate the standard deviation that
defines "outlier". Two robust alternatives:

| Method | Threshold | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Median ± k × MAD** | `median ± k × 1.4826 × MAD` | Barely moved by outliers; one tunable `k` | Assumes roughly symmetric spread |
| **Percentiles** | below p0.5 or above p99.5 | No shape assumption | Always flags ~1% of history by construction |

(`MAD` = median absolute deviation from the median. Multiplying by
1.4826 makes it comparable to a standard deviation for normal data.)

Pick one and write down why. The rest of the lab uses median/MAD.

### 3. Compute thresholds from history (30 min)

Write a query that produces one row per league: `median`, `scaled_mad`,
`lower`, `upper`, plus the season range it was computed from. Commit the
query; generate the CSV from it.

Then **choose `k` with evidence**. For `k` = 4, 5 and 6, count how many
historical games each league would flag:

<details>
<summary>Hint · What the numbers looked like on 2024-25 data</summary>

Regulation-only events per game, flagged games at each `k`:

| League | Median | Scaled MAD | k = 4 | k = 5 | k = 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NBA (1,324 games) | 468 | 28.2 | 7 | 3 | 3 |
| WNBA (312) | 386 | 23.7 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| NCAA men (6,136) | 335 | 47.4 | 91 | 14 | 3 |
| NCAA women (5,473) | 341 | 50.4 | 56 | 22 | 9 |

At `k = 4`, men's college fires 91 times a season: alert fatigue. At
`k = 5`, the NBA hits are the three All-Star mini-games (correct!), and
the college hits are all on the **high** side: fast-paced teams, not
duplicates. Check that yourself: compare `count(*)` with
`count(distinct id)` for the top games.

</details>

### 4. Decide what each side means (20 min)

Low and high outliers mean different things:

- **Too few events:** truncated play-by-play, partial load, game still
  in progress. Usually a real problem.
- **Too many events:** a double load (≈ 2 × median), *or* a genuinely
  fast game. A double load is far beyond any real game, so the high
  threshold can be looser than the low one.

Consider asymmetric thresholds (e.g. low at `k = 5`, high at `2 ×
median`), and a different **severity** for each side. Justify the
choice with the numbers from step 3.

### 5. Test what it can and can't catch (30 min)

Plant three cases per league in a test, as in M9.4:

1. A game with each event duplicated (double load).
2. A game with only the first half of its events.
3. A normal game (must pass).

Run them. Then do the arithmetic for case 2 in men's college: a
half-loaded game has about 335 / 2 ≈ 167 regulation events. Is
`335 − 167` bigger than `5 × 47.4`?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

No: 168 < 237. A volume check with `k = 5` **misses** a half-loaded men's
college game, because college games vary so much. That's not a reason to
lower `k` (back to 91 alerts a season). It's why you also have M9.4's
completeness rule, which catches a game that stops after one half
regardless of volume. Write this up: checks are layers, and you should
know which layer catches which failure.

</details>

✅ **Checkpoint:** double loads caught in all four leagues; half loads
caught in the NBA and WNBA; the men's college gap explained, with the
check that covers it.

### 6. Keep thresholds fresh

Rules change (the NBA changed its clock and pace over the 2002 to 2026
data you have). Decide:

- Which seasons feed the thresholds? (Trailing two seasons is a common
  default; all history mixes eras.)
- How often are they recomputed, and by what? (A monthly Dagster asset
  that rewrites `volume_thresholds` is natural.)
- How does a threshold change get reviewed? (A committed CSV diff in a
  PR is reviewable; a silently updated table isn't.)

### 7. Ship it

Check, threshold query, generated CSV, tests, and the write-up in the PR
body. Results go to `ops.check_results`. `Closes #123`.

## Gotchas

- **Thresholds from data that's already broken.** If history contains
  double loads, they shift the percentiles. Median/MAD resists this; the
  mean doesn't. Dedup history before computing, too.
- **Scope.** Compute thresholds on the games you check (regular season
  and playoffs, no exhibitions), or All-Star games will skew them.
- **Per-team effects.** A few teams always play fast. If high-side noise
  stays a problem, compare a game with *both teams'* trailing averages
  instead of the league's. Note it as a stretch idea; don't build it now.
