# M9.11 · Ready-when check (closed-book)

> **Ticket:** [#129](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/129) ·
> **Mode:** hand, closed-book · **Time:** 45 min ·
> **Needs:** M9.2 to M9.10 done

## Why this matters

The M9 promise: *describe your alerting strategy without causing alert
fatigue.* In an interview this comes as "How do you monitor your
pipelines?" or "Your team gets 200 alerts a week and ignores them. What
do you do?" A list of tools fails this question. A **strategy** passes:
what you alert on, at which severity, through which channel, and how
you keep the noise down.

## The rules

- **No AI, no notes, no dashboard, no repo** until the timer stops.
- Speak out loud, as in an interview. Record it on your phone.
- Use Sideline as your example throughout. Concrete beats generic.

## Steps

### 1. The prompt: 10 minutes, timed

Read this, start the timer, and answer out loud:

> "Walk me through how Sideline's data quality monitoring and alerting
> work. What do you alert on, who gets woken up and when, and how do you
> make sure people don't start ignoring the alerts?"

A complete answer covers, in your own order:

- **Layers:** contracts at the source, rule and volume checks in
  transformation, the freshness SLO on gold, lineage for impact.
- **Symptoms vs causes:** what pages (consumers affected now), what
  becomes a ticket or digest, what's only logged.
- **Severity routing:** how a check's severity maps to a channel.
- **Thresholds from data:** how M9.5's `k` was chosen, with the
  flagged-count evidence.
- **Actionability:** every alert links to a runbook (M6.6) and names an owner.
- **Feedback loop:** how you measure noise (alerts fired vs real
  incidents) and retire or retune checks.
- **One real example:** a chaos-day failure (M9.8), what caught it, how
  fast.

### 2. The follow-ups: 5 more minutes

Answer each in under a minute, still closed-book:

1. "A check fires 3,000 times a season on data that's actually correct.
   What do you do?"
2. "What's the difference between your SLO and an SLA? Why 99% and not
   100%?"
3. "A stat correction for a game last week never reached gold. Which of
   your checks catches it, and which can't?"
4. "How would you know if a check silently stopped running?"

### 3. Score yourself honestly

Play the recording back. Tick each point from step 1 you actually said.

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| All seven points, a real example, follow-ups answered | **Pass.** Close the ticket. |
| Five or six points, or vague on thresholds | Not yet. Reread M9.1 session 4 and M9.5 step 3, retry in two days. |
| A list of tools, no strategy | Redo M9.1 session 4's notes from memory, then retry. |
