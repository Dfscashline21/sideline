# M12.10 · STAR story from your current job

> **Ticket:** [#161](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/161) ·
> **Mode:** hand · **Time:** 45 min ·
> **Prompt:** *A model or analysis at work that was too good to be true,
> or how you'd prevent leakage in your data.*

The STAR format, word budgets and common mistakes are in the
[M0.9 lab](../m0/m0.9-star-story.md). Reread its table before starting.
This lab covers only what's specific to a leakage story.

## Finding the story

The prompt allows two kinds of story. Questions to jog memory:

**"Too good to be true" (a real event):**

- A forecast, churn model or report that looked great in review and
  disappointed in use.
- An analysis joining to a table that was overwritten nightly (a
  customer's *current* segment, status or address) to explain *past*
  behaviour.
- A dashboard metric that changed after the fact because late data or
  corrections rewrote history.
- A test/control comparison where something about the outcome leaked
  into how groups were defined.

**"How you'd prevent it" (a forward-looking story):**

- A table at work that's overwritten in place, where history matters.
  What would you snapshot (SCD2, M5.5) or timestamp so it can be joined
  as-of?
- A feature or report that would need a "known at" timestamp to be
  trustworthy.

If you choose the forward-looking version, be honest that it's a
proposal, and make the Action the concrete steps you've taken or would
take (raised it, prototyped a snapshot, wrote the check).

## What makes a leakage story strong

- **The tell.** What made you suspicious? ("Accuracy was 20 points above
  anything published for this problem.") Interviewers love the moment of
  doubt.
- **The mechanism**, in one sentence: which value was used before it
  could have been known.
- **A number:** accuracy before and after the fix, money or decisions
  that relied on the wrong result, rows or tables affected, time to fix.
- **The prevention:** a test, a snapshot table, a review step, so it
  doesn't recur. That's what separates a senior answer.

## Follow-ups to prepare

Write a one-line answer to each:

1. "How did you convince people the good result was wrong?"
2. "What's your general rule for building training or analysis tables now?"
3. "How would you test for this automatically?" (Your M12.3 test.)

## Where to save it

Private notes alongside your earlier STAR stories, **never** this repo.
Close the issue with "Saved to private notes" and no details.
