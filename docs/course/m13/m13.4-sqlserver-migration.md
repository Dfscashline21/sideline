# M13.4 · Design doc: legacy SQL Server to lakehouse migration

> **Ticket:** [#165](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/165) ·
> **Mode:** hand · **Time:** 4 to 6 hours ·
> **Needs:** M13.1, M5 (dbt), M9 (contracts, reconciliation), M10 (cloud)

## Why this matters

Greenfield designs are rare at work. Migrations are constant, and
they're where careers are made or broken: the new platform is easy, but
moving dozens of reports and hundreds of users **without anyone's numbers
changing or going dark** is hard.

The ticket's three constraints are the whole lesson:

- **Zero reporting downtime**: users never lose a report mid-migration.
- **Dual-run and reconcile**: old and new run side by side until the
  numbers provably match.
- **Rollback**: every step can be undone.

This is also the prompt where "leadership" shows: sequencing, comms,
deciding what *not* to migrate.

## What you'll produce

`docs/design/003-sqlserver-to-lakehouse-migration.md`: 2 to 4 pages,
template, diagram (ideally one per phase), trade-offs.

## Steps

### 1. Describe the starting point (30 min)

Invent a realistic, **generic** legacy estate. (If it resembles your
employer, keep it vague; M13.5 is where your real employer goes, in
private.) Write down, as assumptions:

- database sizes, number of tables, how many have no primary key
- how data gets in today (SSIS packages? nightly stored procedures?
  linked servers?)
- consumers: number of reports/dashboards, BI tool, ad-hoc users, other
  systems reading tables directly
- known pain: slow nightly jobs, no history, licence cost, single server

✅ **Checkpoint:** an inventory table: consumers × the tables they read.
That table is what you'll plan the migration around.

### 2. Target state (30 min)

Map the legacy pieces onto the platform you built: raw zone (M2),
Iceberg (M4), dbt models (M5), orchestration (M6), contracts and checks
(M9), warehouse or serving layer (M10.5, M11). One diagram, before and
after.

### 3. The migration plan in phases (75 min)

The core of the doc. Design phases so that each is **independently
reversible**. Think about:

- **Getting data out:** full extracts, then incremental. Change data
  capture (you did Debezium on Postgres in M8.8; SQL Server has its own
  CDC feature) vs watermark-based pulls (M2.4)?
- **Order of migration:** by consumer, by subject area, or by table? Which
  goes first: the simplest report (a quick win) or the most painful job
  (biggest value)?
- **Cut-over per consumer:** how a report switches from old to new
  source, and how it switches back.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1 · Nudge: the strangler pattern</summary>

Rather than one big cut-over, keep the old system authoritative and add
the new one *beside* it, moving one consumer at a time behind a stable
interface (a view, a semantic-layer model, a BI connection). The old
system shrinks until it's switched off. Each consumer's switch is small
and reversible.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2 · Approach: what dual-run actually means</summary>

For each migrated output, both pipelines produce it from the same inputs
for an agreed period. A reconciliation job compares them daily:

- row counts per key partition (day, league, customer…)
- sums of key measures, with an agreed tolerance
- key-level diffs (`EXCEPT` both ways, exactly as in M1.7)

Define **exit criteria** before you start: e.g. "N consecutive days of
zero unexplained differences". Who signs off?

</details>

### 4. Reconciliation design (45 min)

Write it concretely:

- What gets compared, at what grain, how often
- Tolerances (and why a float sum might differ in the last decimal)
- Where results go (your M9 quality dashboard?) and who gets alerted
- How you classify a difference: new-pipeline bug, old-pipeline bug (it
  happens: the legacy system is often wrong), or expected (e.g. fixed
  logic). Who decides?

### 5. Rollback and risk (40 min)

For each phase: what triggers a rollback, how long it takes, what data
might be lost or duplicated. Then a risk table: likelihood, impact,
mitigation. Include people risks (report owners who distrust the new
numbers) as well as technical ones.

### 6. Trade-offs and cost (40 min)

At least three. Candidates:

| Decision | Options |
|---|---|
| Lift-and-shift vs remodel | Copy tables as-is first, or rebuild with Kimball models (M3)? |
| CDC vs batch extracts | Freshness and load on the source vs complexity |
| Length of dual-run | Confidence vs paying for two platforms |
| Decommission scope | Migrate every report, or retire unused ones (how do you find them?) |

Cost: overlap period running both systems; licence savings after.

## Gotchas

- **Assuming the old numbers are right.** Plan for finding legacy bugs,
  and decide up front who arbitrates.
- **"Big bang weekend cut-over".** It violates zero downtime and makes
  rollback all-or-nothing.
- **Forgetting the readers you don't know about.** Linked servers, Excel
  files with live connections, other apps. How would you discover them
  (query logs, access audits)?
