> **Prompt:** *Influencing a technical decision without authority at work.*

The STAR format, word budgets and common mistakes are in the
[M0.9 lab](../m0/m0.9-star-story.md). Reread its table before starting.
This lab covers only what's specific to an influence story.

## Why this prompt matters

"Tell me about a time you convinced people without being their manager"
is asked in almost every senior loop. It's the behavioural twin of a
design interview: can you get a good design *adopted*?

## Finding the story

Questions to jog memory:

- When did you change a decision another team or a more senior person
  had already made (a tool choice, a schema, a deadline, a process)?
- When did you get people to adopt a practice you introduced: tests, code
  review, a shared model, an incident review?
- When did you *fail* to persuade someone, and what did you learn? A
  failure story with a clear lesson is acceptable and often memorable.

Your M13.5 roadmap may contain one: an initiative you pushed for.

## What makes an influence story strong

- **The other side's view, stated fairly.** "They worried about X, which
  was reasonable because…" shows maturity. Strawmen sound junior.
- **Evidence, not opinion.** A prototype, a benchmark, a cost estimate,
  an incident timeline. Sideline taught you to produce these (M1.7
  timings, M4.8 benchmarks, M10.10 cost cut).
- **The mechanism of influence.** Did you write a design doc or ADR, run
  a pilot, find an ally, make the change cheap to try, or offer a
  rollback? Name it.
- **A number in the result:** adoption (n teams), time or cost saved,
  incidents avoided.

## Follow-ups to prepare

1. "What if they had still said no?"
2. "What would you do differently?"
3. "How did you keep the relationship good afterwards?"

## Where to save it

Private notes alongside your other STAR stories, **never** this repo.
Close the issue with "Saved to private notes" and no details. This story
is one of the eight IP.1 asks for (it fits "design disagreement").
