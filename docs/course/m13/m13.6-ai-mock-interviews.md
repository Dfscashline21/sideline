# M13.6 · Mock system design interviews with an AI interviewer

> **Ticket:** [#167](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/167) ·
> **Mode:** agent: the agent plays the interviewer; *you* do the designing ·
> **Time:** 4 × 75 min (45 min mock + 30 min review) ·
> **Needs:** M13.1 to M13.4

## Why this matters

Writing a design doc alone, with no clock, is very different from
designing out loud while someone interrupts. Interviews test the second.
An AI interviewer can't replace a human, but it's available at 11pm, it
doesn't get bored, and it can play the **sceptical stakeholder** who asks
"why not just use a database?" for the fifth time. That pushback is what
you need to practise.

The agent's role here is the interviewer, never the candidate. If it
starts proposing designs, stop it.

## What you'll produce

- Four timed 45-minute sessions, recorded.
- A review of each in `docs/notes/m13-mocks.md`: scores, top mistake,
  one thing to change next time.

## Steps

### 1. Set up recording (15 min, once)

Record **your voice and screen** (whiteboard and talking). Options: the
built-in screen recorder on your OS, or [OBS](https://obsproject.com).
Keep recordings in a private folder, not the repo. They can be large and
they're yours.

Open a diagram tool you'd use in a real remote interview, such as
[Excalidraw](https://excalidraw.com).

### 2. Write the interviewer prompt (20 min, once)

Write your own, then compare with this starting point:

> You are a senior data engineering interviewer running a 45-minute
> system design interview. The problem is: **<problem>**. Rules:
> - Give me only the one-line problem statement at the start. Reveal
>   requirements only when I ask the right questions; otherwise say
>   "what would you assume?"
> - Every ~10 minutes, push back as a sceptical stakeholder (cost,
>   simplicity, "why not just X?"), and once, change a requirement
>   mid-interview.
> - At minutes 15, 30 and 40, tell me the time.
> - Never suggest designs, components or answers. Ask questions only.
> - At the end, score me 1–5 on: requirements, estimates, data flow,
>   deep dive, trade-offs, failure modes, communication, and give the
>   three most important things to improve, with quotes from what I said.

Save your final version in `docs/notes/m13-mocks.md` so all four sessions
use the same rubric.

<details>
<summary>Hint · Talking to a text interviewer</summary>

If your agent is text-only, speak your answer out loud **and** type a
short summary of each point to the agent. The recording captures how
you'd sound; the typed summary gives the agent something to push back on.
Voice-mode assistants avoid the double work if you have one.

</details>

### 3. Pick four problems

Use two you've written docs for and two you haven't, so you practise
both recall and a cold start:

| Session | Problem | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Real-time betting odds (M13.2) | Familiar: practise performing a known design |
| 2 | Player-tracking pipeline (M13.3) | Familiar, estimate-heavy |
| 3 | A new prompt (e.g. "a ticketing platform's sales analytics" or "a ride-share trip pipeline") | Cold start |
| 4 | Legacy migration (M13.4) or another new prompt | Leadership-heavy |

Space them out: at most one per day, so each review changes the next.

### 4. Run each session (45 min)

- Start the recording and a visible 45-minute timer.
- Follow your M13.1 time budget. Summarise requirements back before
  drawing.
- When challenged, don't cave and don't argue. Restate the trade-off:
  *"We could; we'd gain X and lose Y. Given requirement Z, I'd still…"*
- Stop at 45:00 even if unfinished. Running out of time is data.

### 5. Review it (30 min, ideally next day)

Watch the recording at 1.5x. Fill in:

```markdown
## Session N · YYYY-MM-DD · <problem>
- Agent scores: req _, est _, flow _, deep _, trade-offs _, failure _, comms _
- My own scores (same scale):
- Time at "summarise back": __ min · time at first diagram: __ min
- Filler words / long silences: few / some / many
- Best moment:
- Worst moment (quote yourself):
- One change for next session:
```

**Review the agent too** (the ticket's agent rule). Where was its
feedback wrong or vague? Did it leak hints? Note it. You must be able to
say why you agree or disagree with each point.

✅ **Checkpoint:** session 4's "worst moment" is a different mistake
from session 1's. Same mistake twice means fix that habit before IP.3.

## Gotchas

- **The agent drifts into helping.** If it proposes components, remind
  it of the rule or restart. A helpful interviewer is useless practice.
- **Only reviewing the agent's score.** Your own viewing of the recording
  finds things the agent can't, like pace, filler words and body language.
- **Recordings in the repo.** Keep them private and out of git.
