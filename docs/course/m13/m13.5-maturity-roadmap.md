# M13.5 · Design doc: maturity roadmap for your current employer

> **Ticket:** [#166](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/166) ·
> **Mode:** hand · **Time:** 3 to 4 hours ·
> **Needs:** M13.1; your STAR stories from M0.9 to M12.10

## Why this matters

Senior and staff interviews ask some version of: *"If you joined us and
our data platform were a mess, what would you do in your first year?"*
The best answer is one you've already done the thinking for, about a real
platform: your current one.

Done well, this doc **doubles as your strongest interview story** (the
ticket's words). It shows you can assess a whole system, prioritise, and
sequence change over a year — leadership, not just engineering.

## Privacy first

This is about your employer. The ticket requires it be **anonymised**.

- Write the full version in your **private** notes.
- Commit a public version only if nothing in it could identify the
  company, its systems, its people or its customers: no names, product
  names, internal tool names, exact data volumes, revenue or incident
  details. When in doubt, keep it private. A public copy isn't required.
- Never paste employer documents, schemas, or screenshots into it, or into
  an AI tool.

## What you'll produce

- A private roadmap doc: current state, target state, four quarters of
  steps.
- A two-minute spoken version of it, rehearsed.
- Optionally, a public, fully anonymised version in `docs/design/`.

## Steps

### 1. Assess the current state (60 min)

Use the M13.1 framework's ten areas as a scorecard. For each area, rate
today's platform 1 to 5 and write one line of evidence:

| Area | Score | Evidence (generic wording) |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering hygiene (CI, environments) | | your M0.9 story |
| Ingestion reliability | | M2.10 story |
| Modelling and history | | M3.10 story |
| Transformation (tests, docs) | | M5.15 story |
| Orchestration | | M6.11 story |
| Data quality and incidents | | M9.12 story |
| Security, access, cost | | M10.13 story |
| Serving to consumers | | M11.8 story |
| … | | |

Your module STAR stories are the raw material: most of them describe a
gap in exactly one of these areas.

✅ **Checkpoint:** every score has concrete evidence, not "it's bad".

### 2. Define the target state (30 min)

Where should the platform be in a year? Not "perfect": a realistic
target score per area, chosen for business impact. One paragraph on
**why these areas** matter most to the business.

<details>
<summary>Hint · Prioritise by pain, not by fashion</summary>

For each gap ask: what does it cost today (hours, incidents, wrong
decisions, money), and who feels it? Rank by cost × how many people it
affects. "Migrate to a lakehouse" is rarely quarter one; "stop shipping
wrong numbers" usually is.

</details>

### 3. Plan four quarters (60 min)

For each quarter: goal, two or three initiatives, how you'll measure
success, dependencies, and what you're explicitly **not** doing.

<details>
<summary>Hint · A common shape</summary>

- **Q1 foundations and trust:** CI, tests on the most-used models,
  freshness checks, an incident process. Cheap, fast, builds credibility.
- **Q2 reliability:** idempotent ingestion, orchestration with
  dependencies, alerting without alert fatigue.
- **Q3 scale or modernise:** the platform change the earlier quarters
  made safe.
- **Q4 enablement:** self-service, documentation, data products,
  cost controls.

Use it only if it fits your situation. Order should follow your step 2
ranking.

</details>

### 4. Add the people side (30 min)

A roadmap without people fails. Write:

- who needs to agree (roles, not names), and what each cares about
- how you'd win support without authority (pairs with the M13.8 STAR story)
- one risk per quarter and its mitigation

### 5. Turn it into a story (30 min)

Draft a two-minute spoken version: *"At my current company, the data
platform was … I assessed it across … The biggest cost was … so I
proposed … first, because …"*. Be honest about which parts you have
**done** and which are **proposed**. Interviewers check, and "I proposed
and here's why" is still strong.

Record it once. Over two and a half minutes? Cut.

### 6. (Optional) Public version

If you want a public artifact, rewrite it as "a mid-sized company with a
legacy SQL Server estate" style fiction, removing every identifying
detail, and commit to `docs/design/004-maturity-roadmap.md`. Ask someone
who knows your employer whether it could be recognised. If yes, don't
publish.

## Gotchas

- **Overclaiming.** Don't present proposals as delivered results.
- **Too many initiatives.** Three per quarter, maximum. A roadmap that
  does everything prioritises nothing.
- **No measures.** Each quarter needs a number you'd report (incidents
  per month, time to onboard a source, % of models tested).

## Done when

- [ ] Current state scored across the framework areas, with evidence
- [ ] Target state and four quarters, each with measures and "not doing"
- [ ] People and risk section
- [ ] Kept anonymised: full version private; any public version checked
- [ ] Two-minute spoken version rehearsed and recorded

## Check yourself

1. Why is your Q1 first and not Q3?
2. What would make you change the plan halfway through?
3. Which parts have you actually done, and which are proposals?
