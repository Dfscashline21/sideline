# M13.2 · Design doc: real-time betting odds platform

> **Ticket:** [#163](https://github.com/Dfscashline21/sideline/issues/163) ·
> **Mode:** hand · **Time:** 4 to 6 hours ·
> **Needs:** M13.1 (framework and template), M8 (streaming), M9 (quality)

## Why this matters

This prompt packs three requirements that pull against each other:

- **Under 1 second latency**: odds must reflect the game *now*.
- **Auditability**: for any price ever shown, prove what it was, when,
  and why.
- **Regulatory retention**: keep that evidence for years, cheaply,
  without anyone being able to alter it.

Fast paths want to drop and overwrite; audit wants to keep and never
change. The interview is about how you satisfy both, and the doc is
your practice at writing that down.

You've built most of the pieces already: M8's event-time stream, its
dead-letter topic, its Iceberg sink, and M8.6's live win-probability feed
(odds are close cousins of win probability).

## What you'll produce

`docs/design/001-realtime-betting-odds.md`: 2 to 4 pages, using your
template, with **at least one diagram** and an explicit **trade-offs**
section.

## Steps

### 1. Pin down the requirements (45 min)

Write the questions you'd ask a product owner, then **answer them
yourself as assumptions**, clearly labelled. Areas to cover:

- **Who consumes the odds?** A public website, a mobile app, partner
  feeds, internal traders? Each has a different latency need.
- **What does "under 1 second" measure?** From the on-court event to the
  price on screen? Or from data arriving at you to the price published?
  These differ by the upstream feed delay you can't control.
- **What inputs change a price?** Game events, trader overrides, bet
  volume (liability).
- **What must the audit answer?** E.g. "Show every price for market X
  between 20:41:00 and 20:41:05, and the inputs that produced each one."
- **Retention:** how long, and who may read it? Regulations vary by
  jurisdiction. State the period as an assumption, not a fact.

✅ **Checkpoint:** a requirements table where every non-functional line
has a number (latency target and percentile, events/sec, retention period,
availability).

### 2. Estimate (30 min)

Fill in, with your own assumptions:

- concurrent live games at peak (a busy NBA night, plus college)
- markets per game (moneyline, spread, totals, player props…)
- price updates per market per minute during live play
- bytes per price record with its inputs → **daily and yearly storage**
- read fan-out: how many clients are watching each market?

<details>
<summary>Hint · Which number drives the design?</summary>

Usually **fan-out on the read side**, not write volume. A few thousand
price updates per second is modest for a stream processor. Pushing each
update to many thousands of connected clients within a second is the
hard part: that's a serving problem (WebSockets/SSE, a pub/sub layer, a
CDN edge), not a pipeline problem.

</details>

### 3. Draw the data flow (45 min)

Sketch boxes and arrows from **feed in** to **price on screen** and
**audit record at rest**. Use C4's container level: one box per
deployable thing.

Questions your diagram must answer:

1. Where is the source of truth for "the price at time T"?
2. Which path is the **hot path** (sub-second) and which is the **cold
   path** (audit, analytics)? Do they share a log?
3. Where do trader overrides enter, and are they audited the same way?

<details>
<summary>Hint 1 · Nudge: one log, two consumers</summary>

If every price change is first written to an append-only log (a topic),
then the hot path and the audit path can be two *consumers* of the same
facts. Neither can disagree with the other, because neither is the
source. Compare that with writing to a database and copying it later.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2 · Approach: making audit tamper-evident</summary>

Ideas to evaluate (and choose between) in your trade-offs section:

- Append-only storage with object lock / write-once retention on the
  archive bucket
- Including the input event IDs and model version in every price record
  so a price can be **recomputed** and compared
- Iceberg snapshots for "as of" queries over the archive (you used time
  travel in M4.6). What's their limit for multi-year retention?

</details>

### 4. Trade-offs section (60 min)

Pick at least **three** real decisions. For each: the options, what you
chose, what you give up. Candidates:

| Decision | Options to compare |
|---|---|
| Delivery semantics | At-least-once with idempotent price IDs vs exactly-once processing |
| Late events | Recompute and republish a price vs keep the original and mark it |
| Stream processor | Your M8.10 ADR: does its answer change at sub-second targets? |
| Client delivery | Polling vs Server-Sent Events vs WebSockets |
| Audit store | Same lakehouse as analytics vs a separate locked archive |
| Failure behaviour | If the feed stalls: freeze prices, widen them, or suspend markets? |

The last one is a **business** decision with a data answer. Say who
would decide it and what data they need.

### 5. Quality, failure modes and cost (45 min)

- **Failure modes:** list five ways it breaks (feed outage, duplicate
  events, clock skew, consumer lag, a bad model deploy) and, for each,
  **how you'd detect it** and **what users see**. Reuse M9 ideas:
  freshness SLOs, volume anomalies, dead letters.
- **Cost:** what dominates (always-on streaming compute? retention
  storage? egress to clients?). Rough monthly order of magnitude.

### 6. Review and trim (30 min)

Read it as a busy staff engineer would: is the decision clear in the
first half-page? Cut anything that doesn't help someone decide. Keep it
to 2 to 4 pages.

<details>
<summary>Example outline (structure only, not the design)</summary>

1. Context: live in-game odds, why sub-second matters
2. Requirements: table with p99 latency, peak updates/sec, retention N years (assumed)
3. Estimates: updates/sec, storage/year, client fan-out
4. Design: diagram; hot path; audit path; override path
5. Decisions: 3–4 trade-off tables
6. Failure modes and detection
7. Cost drivers
8. Evolution: more sports, more markets, lower latency
9. Alternatives considered: e.g. database-first with change data capture (M8.8)

</details>

## Gotchas

- **"Exactly-once" as a magic word.** Say what it costs and where it
  stops (it rarely covers the client's screen). Idempotent writes with
  stable IDs are often the honest answer.
- **Inventing regulations.** State retention and access rules as
  labelled assumptions.
- **Only the happy path.** The audit and failure sections are where
  this prompt is actually scored.
